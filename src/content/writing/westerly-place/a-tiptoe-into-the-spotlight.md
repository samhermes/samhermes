Standing in his bedroom, Daniel looked into the mirror at his new outfit for the first day of school. He’d gone on another shopping trip with his uncle to prepare for the big day. The outfit he’d chosen was a pair of black jeans and a purple shirt with a big yellow smiley face on it. He smiled at himself and then grabbed his backpack and headed downstairs.

After having a quick breakfast, Daniel’s uncle drove him to school. His stomach was unsettled the whole way there.

In front of the school, his uncle stopped to let him out. “Okay, have a good first day! I love you!” he called.

“Love you too, see you later!” Daniel got out and waved as his uncle pulled away.

As Daniel approached the school, he saw Alex and Nick walking up. He rushed over to them. “Hey guys!” he said.

“Hey Daniel!” they replied. They were both wearing new clothes too, and they compared their outfits.

Inside the school, Alex and Nick headed off to class and Daniel split off to go to the front office.

“I’ll see you guys at lunch.” He waved them off.

“Okay, we’ll save you a spot!” Nick called back.

Daniel was feeling so confident. Everything felt shiny and new, and he was excited for a fresh start.

In the office, he checked in at the front desk. He was then led on a tour of the school building and shown where his locker was. His first class would be science, and the last stop on the tour was to drop him off at the classroom. It wasn’t crowded yet, so he took a seat in the middle of the room and got out a notebook and pencil.

He watched as the other students came in and took their seats. Then, a group of three guys came in, and as they walked past Daniel, one of them sarcastically said, “Nice shirt.” They all snickered and sat at the back of the room.

Daniel wanted to say something to them, but decided to ignore them. Still, he had to admit that the sheen of the day had already worn off a bit. There had been bullies at his old school, but he’d never been the focus of their attention. He knew that he’d changed over the summer, but didn’t expect it to be so noticeable so quickly.

At lunch, Daniel went through the food line and then looked for Alex and Nick. They were at a round table with stools. Daniel walked over and sat down next to Nick.

“How’s your first day going?” Nick asked.

“Pretty good,” Daniel replied.

“Okay, good.” Nick nodded. “Alex here is somehow already failing math. Can you believe it!?”

Daniel laughed. He looked over at Alex, who was rolling his eyes. There was a window behind him and the light was shining in, giving him a slight glow. Daniel tried not to look too long, but he had a hard time looking away.

“I’m not failing, I just fell for a trick question, okay?” Alex said, annoyed.

“That no one else fell for. You know Mr. Abraham is always trying to play tricks.” Nick laughed.

“I know, I know. I must have gotten rusty over the summer.”

---

Several weeks went by and Daniel settled into the routine of his new school. He had lunch with Alex, Nick, and Catherine most days, and relished the time he got to spend with them.

In mid-September, he started to struggle with some of his homework. In English, he had been assigned *1984* to read. At dinner one night, he complained to his uncle.

“It’s just so boring! I don’t get the point,” he said.

“I think you’ll find that it picks up as it goes on,” Jake replied.

“Maybe. It’s so gross too, it keeps talking about the guy’s nasty ulcer.”

Jake laughed. “Yeah, that part is pretty gross. But it’s a metaphor!”

“So it’s not actually an ulcer?” Daniel asked.

“Well, yes and no, but it’s supposed to be showing you what happens if you don’t question authority. It’s like a physical representation of that.”

Daniel reflected on this. “I thought we were supposed to trust authority. Like, the whole point is that they know what the right thing is to do? ” Daniel set down his fork.

“You should. You should trust authority, but you should also verify that what they’re telling you is true. You should ask questions and make them explain why they’re doing what they’re doing. It’s dangerous to just follow something without question.”

Daniel thought about this for a moment. “This is making me think about my parents,” he said.

Jake chuckled. “Well, that’s a good example. They kicked you out without explanation, right? And you haven’t been able to question them. They completely cut you off.”

Daniel nodded. He still couldn’t believe that his parents had done that to him. However, this new perspective was growing his interest in *1984*, and now he actually wanted to read more.

---

At school a few days later, Daniel was sitting in the hallway between classes having a snack when a group of senior guys walked by. As they passed Daniel, one of them said “gay” just quietly enough that Daniel questioned if he had heard it. He looked up at them just as one of them looked back at him. They were all laughing to themselves.

Daniel had a sinking feeling inside his body. He wasn’t sure what to do, but he wanted to sink into the bench and disappear. He pulled his backpack closer. After a minute, he realized that he had been holding his half-eaten snack in the air and resumed eating.

That night, he recounted what happened to his uncle. Jake listened intently as he described it, then spoke.

“First of all, I’m sorry that that happened to you. That can feel really scary, I know just what it’s like. Second, you can’t let those guys get to you.”

“What do I do though?” Daniel asked.

“You can either ignore it, or you can call them out. Make them say it again. You can respond to them and say ‘so what?’.” Mostly importantly, you don’t want to let them know that it bothered you, or they’ll do it again. Alternatively, if you’re having a bad day, you can always call it back.”

“Uncle Jake, that doesn’t sound like a very good idea.” Daniel smirked.

Jake laughed. “It isn’t. Maybe skip that one.”

“Okay, maybe I’ll try out ‘so what?’.”

---

“Hey, dude, I was wondering if you wanted to watch a movie tonight?”

Jake had come to Daniel’s room and was standing at the door. Daniel was at his desk, doing homework.

“Oh, maybe, I just have some homework to finish. And don’t call me ‘dude’.”

“Okay, well, when you’re done, I’ll be in the living room. Dude.”

Twenty minutes later, Daniel found Jake watching TV. It was an unusual sight. “Okay, I’m ready!” he announced.

“Alright. So, I was thinking maybe you’d like to watch a queer movie with me?” Jake asked.

“Okay, sure.” Daniel sat down on the couch next to him. “What movie?”

“Well, I thought we might start with *Everybody’s Talking About Jamie*, have you heard of that?”

“Nope. I haven’t really heard of any queer movies, actually. Well, I did hear Catherine talking about *Love, Simon*. She said it was ‘super cringe’.”

“Super cringe?! Noo! Is she too cool for it?”

“Probably!” Daniel laughed. “She thinks she’s too cool for a lot of stuff.”

Jake brought up the movie, started it, and then they settled in for the watch. Halfway through, he went and got popcorn for them and they both munched away. As Jamie dealt with his disapproving father, Daniel cried and Jake put his arm around him.

When the credits started to roll, Jake asked, “So, what did you think?”

“That was amazing! I love Jamie.”

Daniel couldn’t put into words exactly what he was feeling, but it was more than just a love for Jamie. He felt a great hope inside himself. The bullies at school *were* ridiculous and sad, and they didn’t matter at all. Daniel wasn’t sure that he was confident enough to be a drag queen, but he felt even more comfortable in his fun clothes now.

Not only that, he was feeling closer to his uncle than he ever had, and a question that had been on his mind for weeks jumped out of him. “Uncle Jake, have you ever dated someone?”

“I have. I dated someone for a long time in my twenties.”

“What happened?”

“Well, we grew apart. Or, I grew up and he didn’t. It just wasn’t good for us anymore, so we broke it off.”

“I’m sorry.”

“It’s okay. It’s just been me ever since, and that’s been good too.”

“You don’t get lonely?”

“I do, sometimes. I have to admit, though, it’s been really nice to have you here.”

Daniel smiled with melancholy. He hugged his uncle. “What about coming out? When did you come out?”

“Gosh, that’s a whole other story.” He rotated in his seat to face Daniel. “I didn’t come out, exactly. I was reading about being gay online and your mom came in. I couldn’t hide the webpage fast enough and she made me show her. I begged her not to tell Grandma and Grandpa, but she wouldn’t listen.”

Daniel furrowed his brow. “She made you come out?”

“Yep. She told them what I’d been reading, and I had to show it to them too. It was so embarrassing and humiliating. I hadn’t even figured out what it meant yet. You know, that’s why I was reading about it.”

“What happened then?”

“Well, they grounded me for a while and then they started surveilling my every move. I couldn’t have a computer in my room anymore and they started reading all of my texts. It was a nightmare.”

“Oh my gosh. I can’t believe they did that.”

“I can. And your mom clearly hasn’t learned anything from it. Or, she learned the wrong things.”

“Yeah. I guess she’s always been like this.” Daniel sighed. “She’s just a terrible person.”

Jake sighed. “When you showed up here, it wasn’t a surprise to me.”

“Wait, really? Why not?” Daniel looked concerned. “Did you... did you know that I was gay?"

“No, no, I didn’t know for sure. But I knew your mom, and I had an inkling about you.”

“An inkling? What’s an inkling?”

“Just a feeling. When you all came here to visit me when you were younger, you were really interested in the disco ball that I had on my bookcase.”

“I forgot about that! I loved that thing. Wait, where is it?” Daniel glanced around the room.

“It’s in my bedroom now.”

“I need to see it again.”

Jake laughed. “I’ll go get it in a minute.” After a beat, he asked, “I was going to ask you, have you asked Alex out on a date yet?”

Daniel immediately blushed and sat back on his seat. “Ugh, no, not yet.”

“What’s holding you back?”

“I don’t know what to say. It’s just so overwhelming.“

“You should see if he wants to go to the café for dinner some night. You know, the place where you both met, somewhere familiar.”

Daniel smiled at his uncle. “That’s actually a really good idea.”

“I know. Ask him tomorrow.”

“Okay, fine. I’ll ask him tomorrow.”

---

During science class the next morning, Daniel’s mind was buzzing with ideas of how he could ask Alex out. He thought about meeting him at his locker, or cornering him after school. He thought about asking him at lunch, but Nick would be there. He finally decided that he would just find him in the hallway and try to be really casual about it.

After science, he searched for Alex. He paced up and down the hallway, hoping to be in the right spot at the right time, but he never saw him, and it nearly made him late to math. At lunch, he sat with Alex, but didn’t bring up the question. Later, on the way to English, he saw Alex, but he dipped into a classroom before Daniel could get there.

Finally, after school, Daniel had had enough. He saw Alex heading for the busses and he ran over to him.

Out of breath, he said, “Hey Alex, wait!” Alex stopped and turned to look at him. Daniel continued, “Hey, um, can I ask you a question?”

Alex looked curious. “Yeah, what?”

Daniel realized that Alex could miss his bus. “Oh, um, if you need to go, it’s okay.”

“Yeah, my bus is leaving soon, I can’t miss it.”

“Okay yeah, go ahead, it’s okay, it was nothing.” Daniel motioned for him to go.

“Well now I need to know what it was. Just ask me!”

“Okay fine, fine!” Daniel paused for dramatic effect. “Will you go on a date with me?"

Alex looked shocked and covered his mouth with his hands. “Oh. Oh, I mean, yes.” He laughed. “Of course. Yeah, let’s go on a date.”

“Okay, sweet.” Daniel hadn’t imagined this part of the conversation.

At this, Daniel looked over at Alex’s bus to see that the door was closing. “Oh no!”

Alex followed Daniel’s eyes and spun around. “Shoot, I gotta go.” He ran after the bus and tried to flag it down, only for the bus to take off without him.

Daniel ran to catch up to Alex. “I’m so sorry! That was all my fault.”

“I’m never going to make it home in time now. Ugh!” Alex threw his hands up and let them flop at his sides.

“I’m sorry! I was going to ask you earlier but then I ran out of time and panicked. And now here we are.”

“Don’t worry, I can figure something out. Wait, you were going to ask me earlier? Why didn’t you? I saw you like ten times today.”

Daniel put his hands over his face. “Ugh, I know, I just chickened out.”

Alex laughed. “You’re so goofy. I’ve been wanting to ask you out too.”

“Wait. Really?”

“Really.”

“Oh. Well why didn’t you?”

Alex tried to brush this off, but it was clear that he had also been scared to ask. “We don’t need to get into that now. I need to find a way to get home.”

“Oh, right. I can ask my uncle if he can take you home?”

“Really? He would do that?”

“Of course. He should be here in a minute.”

When Jake arrived, Daniel asked him and then they both climbed into the back seat.

“Hey Alex, good to see you again,” Jake said.

“You too. Thanks for this, I would’ve made my bus if Daniel here hadn’t held me up.”

“Oh? What was he holding you up for?” Jake smirked at them through the rearview mirror.

Daniel was quiet but his eyes darted around. He tried to avoid Alex’s eyes, but was unsuccessful.

Alex smiled. “He was asking me out.”

“Oh, was he now?” Jake asked. He looked over to Daniel and back to Alex. “And what did you say?”

“I said ‘yes,’” Alex replied, and nudged Daniel’s shoulder.
