Westerly Place was a utopia. A suburban village where everything was planned and proper and prudent. Nothing was ever out of place, at least not for long, and the people behaved in orderly and routine ways. Families could live there and be safe from any harm. Those that did could never imagine leaving, and so they didn’t.

The Walker family lived in Westerly place, with two children, a boy and a girl. The Walkers loved living in Westerly Place. Each of the homes were plain in style, with matching black roofs and coordinating beige siding. Everyone who lived there drove an SUV in black, white, or beige. No one had any pets because they would cause too much of a mess.

The Walkers made sure that their clothing was plain as well. They didn’t want to stand out from their neighbors, so they wore the same colors as everyone else. A common outfit was brown pants, a beige shirt, and white shoes. Each day, they cycled different combinations of these colors. Sometimes their shoes were brown, and on those days they made sure to wear beige pants so as not to upset the balance.

Mrs. Walker made all of the food for the family, which she carefully scheduled each week. She varied combinations of chicken, rice, and beans. She always included a salad with dinner, but refrained from putting too many toppings on it. She did not want to seem adventurous, or else she might have felt out of control. She hated to feel out of control.

The Walker children were Sarah and Daniel. They each had their own room, set with only the basics, and they were to keep them neat and tidy. For fun, they were allowed to play chess. They could not read books that were not assigned to them for school, and they did not get to watch TV or movies. Their parents would not allow them to have a computer or a smartphone, either. They played a lot of chess.

Mr. Walker worked in finance and left each morning in his white SUV at exactly 7:15am. Each night, Mrs. Walker packed him a lunch for the next day, and he returned home at exactly 5:45pm each night for dinner. Mr. Walker did not talk about his work, and the rest of the family did not ask. In the evenings, Mr. Walker would wind down by using his rowing machine in the basement, and he did not like to be disturbed.

The Walkers were very happy in Westerly Place. Nothing bad had ever happened since they moved in, and nothing bad ever would happen. It was not like other places, such as the city, where things did happen. Innocent people had things happen to them in cities, and the Walkers did not want to become one of them.

Despite their best intentions, the Walkers forgot to account for one thing. They had forgotten that life itself is uncontrolled. Over time, Westerly Place had lulled them into a sense of freedom from this truth, and this had put them in an incredibly fragile spot. No amount of planning or seclusion could keep them from the eventual event. Unbeknownst to them, it had already been set in motion long before they had ever had a chance to even imagine it.

Daniel Walker loved chess. He was certain of this, because he played it nearly every day with his sister. He had gotten quite good at it over time, but his sister had as well. Their games were sometimes intense, as they each fought to outsmart the other. Their parents often reprimanded them for getting too loud when they played, so they had learned to scream internally.

Daniel also loved school. He was certain of this as well, and he delighted getting straight A’s on his report card. This made his parents quite pleased, though there was never a reward or celebration of any kind. It would have been too silly and out of line to do so. Daniel spent most nights in his room, reading his textbooks. He often read ahead of the assignment, as he had the extra time and he liked to be ahead.

Last fall, Daniel had gotten his drivers license, and his parents had bought him a small car. He enjoyed driving to school with his sister. On the way, he often quizzed her to prepare her in case of pop quizzes. Sarah also had straight A’s, and she did not want to fall behind. Once, on a drive, Daniel had accidentally turned on the car radio. Sarah had jumped to turn it off as quickly as possible. She had been relieved, but Daniel had had an uneasy feeling. He had enjoyed the dance music that he heard, but pretended to be relieved as well.

It was now nearing the end of the school year and Daniel and Sarah were busy studying for their final exams. They never stressed about finals week in the way that other students did, but they remained focussed on preparing in every way that they could. Nothing bad had ever happened, and they didn’t have any reason to expect otherwise.

On the second to the last Thursday of the school year, Daniel was at the tail end of his PE class. They had been playing basketball and most of the class had already began to head to the lockers. Before he followed, he picked up a basketball that hadn’t been put away, and took it into the equipment room. There, he noticed his friend Oliver, who was hanging up a jump rope. Daniel put the basketball on the rack with the others, and when he turned around to leave, he noticed that Oliver was motioning him over.

As Daniel approached, Oliver took his hand, and then led him behind a storage rack. Daniel was confused about why he was acting this way, as he’d never held his hand before. Oliver turned to face Daniel, and after a bit of hesitation, quietly asked, “Do you want to kiss me?” Daniel wasn’t sure what to say, and turned to check that no one else was in the room. “It’s okay,” Oliver said. Daniel stared into Oliver’s eyes for a moment, then leaned in and they quickly kissed. After their eyes opened again, a slight smile appeared on Oliver’s face. Daniel could only stare, still in shock about what was happening. He felt that something was wrong, deeply wrong, about what they had just done, but he also knew that he had really liked it. He had never thought about kissing Oliver before.

Oliver waited, unsure what Daniel was going to do. Then, Daniel leaned back in and kissed him again. They kissed, awkwardly, sloppily, for just a little bit, and then Daniel turned around and rushed out of the room. He didn’t stop to look back at Oliver, he just left. He expected Oliver to follow him, but he did not.

Daniel’s mind was fuzzy for the rest of the day. He couldn’t find words for what he was feeling. He wanted to get in his car and drive far away, but another part of him wanted to find Oliver again. He didn’t know what he would do when he found Oliver, but he knew that it seemed right. He wanted to talk to him about what had happened, but didn’t begin to know what he would say.

The next week, final exams began. Daniel was still very distracted, and struggled to remember everything that he had studied. For his math final, he ran out of time and had to turn in the exam with some of the questions still blank. He had tried hard to finish, but it felt as though he was taking a senior level exam. He was terrified that his grades would slip down to B’s, but he couldn’t do anything about it.

He saw Oliver a few times, but did his best to avoid him. He got the feeling that Oliver was avoiding him as well, and he was both relieved and confused at this. He hoped that he might run into him in a quiet spot, but he never did. After a while, he thought of putting a note in his locker, but he became increasingly convinced that Oliver hated him. Surely he would not want to get a note from him.

Mr. and Mrs. Walker did not notice that Daniel was struggling. They did not often talk to their children or ask them questions about their day. Daniel retreated to his room each night. When Sarah stopped by to play chess, he shrugged her off, saying that he needed to study more. Instead, he spent many evenings laying in bed, staring at the ceiling, trying to figure out what had happened. He tried reading books, but the words kept blurring together and he found that he couldn’t understand what they were trying to say.

After all of the finals were done and Daniel and Sarah had been released for summer break, they received their report cards. Sarah presented hers to her parents, and they were very pleased. They then asked to see Daniel’s and he reluctantly handed it over. He had already seen it, and there were several B’s. His parents were outraged at the sight and berated him for laziness and irresponsibility. They yelled about his future, which they claimed had been ruined. They couldn’t believe what had happened. Nothing was supposed to happen. Daniel was a failure of the worst kind.

Now, not only was Daniel confused about the kiss, he was also ashamed of what he had done to his report card. He felt that it was all his fault, he was certain of this. He spent several days in his room alone, doing nothing in particular. His mind remained fuzzy. On a Tuesday afternoon, he decided to go for a run. He hoped that it would clear his mind, they way that it had in PE. He dressed in beige athletic wear and put on white shoes. He left the house and ran down to the trail at the edge of the village.

Daniel ran several miles and got very sweaty. He felt disgusting and wished that he was clean. As he approached a small park, he decided to sit down to cool off for a bit. There, he sat and watched as children played on the equipment. He thought about everything that had happened. His mind was a little more clear than it had been. He tried to think of what to do to fix everything. He wanted desperately to put everything back together the way that it had been before.

It was then that he realized what he needed to do. He needed to tell his parents about the kiss. He had done nothing wrong, and he knew that they would fix it. He didn’t know how, but they would know. They knew many things, and even though this had never happened before, he knew that they would know what to do. He would tell them and it would never happen again and he could get back to normal.

That night, after dinner, Daniel asked to speak to his parents. They dismissed Sarah to her room and they each sat at the table, expectantly looking at Daniel. He fidgeted in his seat a bit, and then spat it out. A boy had kissed him. He explained that he’d been putting away a basketball and then Oliver had kissed him. He hadn’t even done anything, he lied, Oliver had done it all.

His parents were hysterical. They yelled and yelled. They blamed Daniel for what had happened. They couldn’t believe that not only had he gotten bad grades, now he had kissed a boy. It was unconscionable, they said. Unbelievable, his dad exclaimed. His mother maintained a disgusted look on her face the entire time. As soon as they quieted down, they found something else to yell about.

Daniel sat, horrified, as it became clear that they were not going to help him at all. He felt much worse about the kiss, and wished even more that it had never happened. If his parents couldn’t handle half of the truth, he knew that they certainly couldn’t handle it all, and that made him feel completely helpless.

At the end, after they had finished yelling, his dad told him that he had to leave. He sounded calm at first, but as Daniel protested, he grew louder. His mom agreed. He needed to leave the house and he couldn’t come back. They didn’t care where he went, just that he needed to leave. They told him that he could go to his room to get his things, but that after that, he had to leave.

In his room, tears streamed down Daniel’s face. He hadn’t even noticed that he had started crying. He was beginning to go numb. He gathered his clothes and threw them into his backpack, dripping tears on them as he went. He went back to his parents, and they led him to the front door and opened it for him. They didn’t say anything, just motioned, and he had no choice but to go outside.

Without thinking, Daniel got into his car and started it, tears still streaming down his face. He hoped that none of the neighbors had seen him, as he didn’t want them to talk. He felt deeply ashamed that this was happening, and he was certain that it was his fault too. Everything had been his fault. No one else in Westerly Place had these things happen to them. He put the car in reverse and slowly backed out of the driveway. He made sure to drive cautiously, as he had been taught, but he couldn’t see the road very well. At the end of the neighborhood, he turned left, unsure where he was going.
