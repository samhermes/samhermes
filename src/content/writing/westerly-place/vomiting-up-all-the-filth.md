Weeks went by and Daniel settled into the routine of living with his uncle. Every day, Jake would come home and make them lunch. In the evenings, they would play board games and watch TV together. The shows were shocking to Daniel, and he often felt unsettled when they were over. Some evenings, they would go on walks around the nearby park. During those, Daniel would watch as the other kids played basketball or volleyball, wishing he could be a part of it.

Despite all of this, Daniel found a new level of boredom. While his uncle was at work, he struggled to find ways to keep himself busy. There was no schoolwork to catch up on or chess to play with his sister. He tried reading some of his uncle’s books, but they were difficult to follow. Usually, he would give up and just nap on the couch or lay in his bed.

Combined with the steady supply of delicious food that his uncle was cooking, Daniel soon found himself gaining weight. This fact disturbed him greatly, as it had never happened before. It made him feel out of control. He inspected his belly in the mirror each morning, feeling a new kind of despair. His parents had often spoken of the possibility of this happening, and never in good terms.

Daniel’s uncle seemed intent on indulging him, baking more brownies, as well as other sweets like cookies and cake. He felt that his uncle’s heart was in the right place, but each day made him feel worse and worse. He couldn’t find a way to tell his uncle to stop, and they always ate together at the kitchen table, his uncle watching his every move. He wasn’t sure how his uncle managed to stay trim.

It was a day near the end of June when it all came to a head. Usually, Daniel would be downstairs when his uncle arrived for lunch, but on this day, his uncle had to come and found him in his bedroom. He was facing away, and didn’t stir when his uncle greeted him.

“Don’t you want to come have a bit of lunch?”

“Not today,” Daniel mumbled. He didn’t move.

Jake put his hands on his hips. He thought about what he could say, but decided to leave Daniel to himself.

Once his uncle left, Daniel rolled onto his back to stare at the ceiling. He tried not to look at his belly, just out of eyesight. He entertained the idea of returning home to his parents. He wondered if they would take him back, if it had been enough time. He missed the structure of the days there.

That night at dinner, Jake prodded him for details. “Do you want to talk about what you’re feeling? I’m here to listen, I know that this hasn’t been easy.”

Daniel pushed the carrots around on his plate. He sighed. “I miss home.”

Jake nodded. “That’s understandable.”

“I want to go back.”

Jake took this in for a moment. “I’m not sure that you can. At least not yet. Your parents were pretty firm.”

Daniel began to tear up and hung his head.

“That’s not your only option though. I’ve been giving you some space so far, but maybe you need to get out of the house more? Does that sound good?”

“Yeah, maybe.”

“I know the owner of the little café down the street, and she’s always looking for a little extra help. Want me to see what I can do?”

Daniel looked up at his uncle. His eyes were red, but he looked hopeful. He nodded, slowly. “Sure. Thanks.”

“Don’t be too hard on yourself, your whole world got turned upside down in a single day. There’s always the option to change things though, you just let me know, okay?”

Daniel nodded.

“Is anything else bothering you? Something on your mind?”

Daniel searched his plate for words. This was a great time to tell his uncle that he was bothered by his weight, but he wanted to be as gentle as possible. His uncle had been so nice to make him all kinds of good food.

“Yeah, um, I was thinking that I should maybe have fewer sweets.”

“Fewer sweets? Yeah, of course, we can cut back. Any reason why?”

Daniel furrowed his brow. “Um, it’s my belly.”

Jake instantly understood, and felt bad for causing him distress. He’d felt a lot of his own shame around his weight, and it occurred to him that Daniel’s parents had probably been strict about it. He reached his hand across the table and opened it. “Is that making you sad?”

Daniel nodded and took his uncle’s hand.

“Hey, that’s okay. I just wanted to help you feel better, but if that’s not what you need, I’m sorry. I saw how excited you were about those brownies the day you got here and I might have overdone it.”

Daniel looked guilty. “I’m sorry, I really did like them.”

“No, don’t be sorry. We’ll take a little break, save them for special occasions.” He took his hand back from Daniel’s and looked over at the counter, where a container of cookies sat. He thought to put them in the freezer later, then tried to figure out what to say next. Finally, he asked, “Hey, can we talk about what gaining weight means to you?”

“Okay...” Daniel replied.

“How does it make you feel?”

“Well, bad, like I’m a bad person.”

“Okay, and that makes you feel like you messed up? And like there’s nothing you can do to fix it?”

Daniel nodded. “Yeah, exactly. It feels like it’s permanent.”

“I get that. You know that it’s okay to gain weight though, right? There’s no shame for putting on weight. It’s just something that happens sometimes.”

Daniel furrowed his brow. This was a new thought.

“I don’t know what your parents have told you, but whatever your body looks like is perfect. Now, if you’ve gained weight because of too many sweets or not enough exercise and you want to change that to feel better, that’s up to you. But however your body looks, you’re still you. I still love you. You need to make sure that you still love yourself too.”

Daniel began tearing up again. The walls of shame he’d built up over the past couple of weeks began to crack. “Thanks.” He rubbed his eyes and tried to avoid eye contact.

“I mean it.”

“I know.”

“Okay, on that note, we’ll skip the cookies tonight and you can let me know if you want to have one in the future.”

“Sounds good.”

Several days later, Jake came with good news. He’d secured a job for Daniel at the café, starting on the following Monday. He delivered the news over lunch and Daniel excitedly took it all in. He felt that things were finally looking up.

“Before you start, I was wondering if you wanted to get some new clothes? You can keep borrowing mine, but maybe we get something more your size?” asked Jake.

Daniel brightened. “Yeah! That would be fun. I like what your clothes look like though, maybe we could get things like them?”

“Yeah? You want to try out something a little more exciting?”

Daniel laughed. “Yeah. My clothes are so boring.”

That night after dinner, Daniel and Jake went to the mall and wandered in and out of different stores. Daniel struggled to take in all of the bright displays and options, and the music made it hard to focus. In one store, Daniel picked up a red shirt with a pink wave pattern. He pondered it, drawn in to its bold statement. Jake watched, hoping not to scare him off.

“That one’s nice,” Jake said.

“Yeah. I like it. I think it might be too much though.”

“Maybe... or maybe it’s just enough.”

Daniel looked at Jake and smiled. “Okay, I want to get it.”

They picked up pairs of pants in different colors and found several pairs of shoes with colorful accents.

At an underwear rack, Jake paused. “I don’t want to make this awkward, but maybe you’d like to get some? You don’t have very many pairs and I’ve been having to do laundry all the time.”

“Okay. Umm, I don’t see the kind I usually get though.”

“Oh, right, I don’t see any briefs here. Would you be open to trying something different?” He took a pair off of the rack. “They have these, they’re called boxer briefs. They’re really comfortable.”

“Sure. But could I maybe try some like you wear?”

“Oh, boxers? Yeah, we can get you some of those. Let’s see what they have.”

They looked through the boxers and picked out different pairs with bright prints and patterns on them. Daniel laughed at a pair with a banana pattern on it.

Before they headed to the register, Daniel stopped and looked at everything they had gathered. “Are you sure this is okay? This is a lot of stuff, it’s going to be expensive. Maybe we should just get some basics instead?”

“No, it’s okay, I want to buy them for you. If you’re having second thoughts, we don’t have to get any of it. But don’t put anything back because you’re scared. If you think these will be fun to wear, listen to that voice.”

Daniel stood, stunned. He wasn’t used to adults talking to him like this. His uncle was so free and firm in himself. Daniel couldn’t even question it, it came to his uncle so naturally. It felt right to him too. There was a nagging voice in the back of his head that was telling him to stop, but he was curious to know what would happen if he didn’t.

When they arrived back at the house, they went upstairs so that Daniel could try on some of the new clothes. Jake waited in his bedroom while he changed in the bathroom. Daniel put on a pair of light green pants and the red and pink shirt. He came into the bedroom, looking apprehensive.

“Look at you! Wow, you look fantastic!” Jake exclaimed as he took it in.

Daniel blushed and went to the floor length mirror to check himself out. The shirt looked so bright on him. He’d never worn anything like it before. The fabric was really soft though, and he liked how it fit. He wondered what people would think about him. More precisely, he thought about what his parents would think. They would probably make him change.

He turned around to face his uncle, who was smiling expectantly.

“So, what do you think?” Jake asked.

“I don’t know. It’s kind of a lot.” Daniel laughed nervously.

“Yeah, but it’s fun. Again, no pressure, but if you like how you feel in them, don’t let anything else get in your way.”

“Okay. You’re right. They are really comfy. I think I just need to get used to them a little bit.”

Daniel tried on several other outfits and showed them off to his uncle, enjoying it more each time.

On Sunday night, Jake had a group of friends over. He cooked a big meal and they all sat around the table in the kitchen to eat. Daniel joined them, and listened in as they animatedly swapped stories. They talked about things that Daniel had never heard of before, and he struggled to keep up. Sometimes, it was like they were speaking a different language. He was entranced.

After dinner, they moved to the living room and gathered around the TV. Jake brought out drinks and soon the living room was rowdy. Daniel felt left out without a drink, and the stories only got more indecipherable.

Looking for an escape, Daniel wanted to tell his uncle that he was going to go to his room, but he couldn’t find a good moment to say it. He didn’t want to bring down the energy in the room either. He finally realized that he could just claim he was going to the bathroom and not come back. He stood up, mumbled where he was going, and slipped away upstairs.

In his room, he sat on the edge of his bed. He tried to breathe to calm down, but it didn’t work very well. He looked to the side and caught his reflection in the mirror. He looked at his belly. His uncle had cut down on the sweets, but his belly hadn’t changed. Even though it had only been a week, he felt helpless.

Daniel was crying when his uncle came in. He tried to hide his face as soon as he saw him, but it was too late.

“Hey, what’s going on? Are you okay?” Jake came to the bed and slowly sat down next to him.

Daniel wiped at his face. “It’s nothing.”

“I’m sorry, whatever it is. Are my friends too much?”

“No, they’re fine.” His uncle rubbed his back slowly. No one had ever done that to him before, and he had to admit that it helped.

“Okay, well, let’s talk after my friends leave. They’re only going to be here a little longer, the show is almost over. Does that sound good?”

Daniel nodded, and Jake got up and left the room. Daniel sighed and laid down on the bed. He didn’t know what he was going to talk about with his uncle, he didn’t think that there was anything wrong. He nodded off a bit and only woke when Jake returned.

Downstairs, in the kitchen, Jake put away the dishes and cleared off the kitchen table. Then he sat down across from Daniel with a tray of brownies. He dished up two of them and slid one across to Daniel.

“I’m sorry, I can’t tonight.”

“Are you still feeling bad about your weight? It’s been a week since you’ve had any sweets. You can have one every once in a while.”

Daniel contemplated this. He knew his uncle was being rational, but his mind was all jumbled up about what was right and wrong. “No, you’re right. I think I still need to take a break.”

“Okay, that’s okay, no pressure. Just don’t beat yourself up over this. It takes time.”

Daniel was relieved that he didn’t have to eat the brownie. He felt bad for rejecting it though.

“So, can we talk about what you were feeling earlier? Are you sure my friends weren’t too much?”

“No, they were fine, I’m sorry. I think I was just feeling things.”

“Yeah, just overwhelmed? That happens. Tomorrow is a big day too, first day at your new job.”

“Yeah, I know, I’m kind of nervous.”

“That’s understandable. It’s going to be a lot to take in, but you’ll get the hang of it before too long.”

Daniel nodded and looked down at his hands. After a moment, he said, “There’s something that I didn’t tell you.”

Jake set down his fork. “Okay...”

Daniel hesitated, unable to find the words for what he wanted to say. His uncle had shown him that it was safe for him to tell the truth, but it was still hard to say out loud. Finally, he decided to keep it simple.

“I kissed a boy.”

Jake looked at him with a neutral expression, waiting for him to say more.

“It’s the reason why my parents kicked me out. I told them about it and they absolutely freaked out and screamed at me and then kicked me out.”

Jake furrowed his brow and slightly nodded, hoping that he would continue.

“I didn’t even mean to kiss him, I was in the equipment room at school and before I knew it, we were kissing.”

Jake waited again, and when it appeared that Daniel was done, he said, “Thank you for telling me.”

Daniel nodded. He still wasn’t sure what his uncle was thinking, and it unsettled him.

“First of all, I love you. No matter what. I don’t know what you’ve been told about kissing boys, but that’s normal in this house. In fact, we encourage kissing boys here.”

Daniel laughed, more than he meant to. The pressure escaped from him.

“Second, I’m sorry that that is the reason your parents kicked you out. That was absolutely the wrong thing for them to do, and I hope they come to regret that. That’s no reason to reject someone and I’m glad that you came here.” He got up from the table and went around to Daniel and took him in a big hug. Daniel squeezed him back, relieved that he’d finally shared the truth and that it had been so well received.

They released from the hug and Jake said, “I’m also sorry that you haven’t been able to see that boy again. Was it someone you liked?”

“Yeah.” Daniel choked on his words. “It was my friend Oliver. We’ve been friends forever, but I never thought about kissing him. He just kind of did it.”

“Did you kiss him back?”

Daniel nodded.

“Well, I hope we find you a way to see Oliver again. I’m sorry you’ve been separated from him too, that’s really hard.”

“Thanks. I hope so too, I miss him a lot.”

“I bet so. Thank you again for telling me, I know it isn’t easy.”

Daniel nodded. “Well, I better go to bed.”

“Okay, well, I’ll see you in the morning. I love you.”

“I love you.”

As Daniel laid in bed, he felt clearer than he had in weeks and it seemed like it was easier to breathe. He replayed what his uncle had said, and reveled in how supportive that he had been. It made him want to stay at his uncle’s forever. His house was beginning to feel more like home than anywhere he’d ever been.

Soon, he drifted off to a peaceful, deep sleep.
