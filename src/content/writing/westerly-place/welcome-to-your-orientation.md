On the first Monday of July, Daniel and Jake started with their usual morning routine. Daniel got dressed in some of the new clothes that they had bought together, choosing a dark blue pair of pants with a green t-shirt. They had breakfast together, and then Jake left for work. An hour later, Daniel set off for his first day at the café. He was to arrive an hour before it opened for orientation.

At the café, Daniel tried the front door, only to find that it was locked. He peered in through the window, but didn’t see anyone, and all of the lights were off. He looked around the corner of the building, but didn’t see anyone there either. He wandered towards the back of the building, but all he found there was a dumpster.

Back at the front of the building, he sat down on a nearby bench and decided to wait and see if someone showed up soon. He watched as other people milled about the area, and he wondered where they were headed. He was fascinated by people who lived in the city. They dressed so differently and all seemed to know something Daniel didn’t.

After ten minutes, Daniel began to question if his orientation had been cancelled or rescheduled. His parents had taught him that it was incredibly rude to be late, but he tried to relax. Surely someone would appear soon. He turned to look inside the café, and to his surprise, the lights were on. He got up from the bench and went to the front door. Still, it was locked, so he peered through the front window again.

Inside, a woman was rushing towards the door. She had long, dark brown hair and was wearing a light wash pair of overalls. When she got there, she unlocked it, opened it, and then said, “You must be Daniel, hello!”

“Hello, that’s me! I’m sorry, I don’t think I know your name.”

“I’m Brit, it’s so nice to meet you! Come on in!”

Daniel followed her inside and they walked to the back of the dining room. The building was made of brick, which had been left exposed inside. The floor was dark stained hardwood, and the tables and chairs matched. On the walls were large, colorful abstract paintings that each had a small spotlight.

They went through a door into the kitchen area, where Brit led Daniel on a tour of all the back of house spaces. At the end, she showed him where he could leave his stuff and pick up an apron. He was to be a waiter, which excited him. Next, Brit showed him how to put orders into the computer, and then walked him through some etiquette.

Soon, other employees began arriving and they went about preparing for opening. Daniel watched them, fascinated by the easy way they moved around. There was to be one other waiter working, and they arrived just before opening. Daniel nearly ran into him as he burst into the kitchen.

“Oh, hey, I’m Alex.”

“Hey, I’m Daniel.” Daniel shook his hand, awkwardly. He wasn’t sure how to greet people. Alex appeared to be a few years older than Daniel.

“Thank god you’re here, I’ve been doing this by myself for forever.” Alex put his bag down and grabbed his apron and began tying it.

“Oh, yeah, sure. I think I’m just here for the summer, so sorry if it’s not long enough.”

“Hey, no worries, I get it. Nothing is forever.”

For the first day, Daniel was to follow Alex around and observe how he was doing the job. Throughout the morning, he watched as Alex bantered with regulars and recalled previous orders from memory. He wasn’t sure he would ever be that good.

At lunchtime, two customers came in that were around Alex’s age and sat at a table in the back. As Alex and Daniel approached them, they threw jokes at Alex and mocked his outfit. Daniel realized that they must be Alex’s friends.

“Who’s your friend here, Alex?” the girl asked.

“Oh, this is Daniel,” Alex replied. “He just started today, he’s shadowing me for now.”

“Cute, I love your shirt, Daniel,” she said.

Daniel had forgotten what he was wearing, so glanced down. “Oh, thanks. My uncle bought it for me.”

“I love that. My uncle never buys me anything, he’s such a tightwad. Is your uncle gay?”

Daniel immediately blushed. “Oh... um, I don’t know.”

“Well, either way, he’s got great taste.”

Daniel couldn’t believe that someone would just ask something like that so openly. His mind felt fuzzy as he questioned if his uncle was gay. He hadn’t thought about it before, but it kind of made sense. Alex and his friends were so wild and carefree to him, it made him want to run away but he was also curious to see what else they would say.

Daniel’s shift ended at 2pm, and he sat down with Alex to eat their shift food.

“So, what’d you think? Are you going to come back tomorrow?” Alex asked.

Daniel laughed. “Yeah, of course.”

“Good. We didn’t scare you off. Oh, and sorry about my friends, they can be a bit much.”

“No, that’s okay, they’re really nice.”

“Well, that’s just not true, they’re the rudest people I’ve ever met. But I love them anyway.”

Daniel laughed, and then there was quiet as they each took a bite of their sandwich. Daniel tried to think of an interesting question to ask, but he was coming up empty. He felt a slight panic.

“So, what’s your whole deal?” Alex asked.

Daniel swallowed. “My deal?”

“Yeah, like you’re so intense and stuff.”

“Am I?” Daniel’s slight panic grew into a full panic. He shifted in his seat. Alex continued to eat, seemingly unaware of the effect he had had.

“It’s okay, just saying. We’re all chill here, so no worries.”

“Yeah, right.” Daniel had no idea what he was talking about, but was relieved Alex had let it go.

Daniel shifted the conversation to work after this, and they talked about how some customers have really weirdly specific preferences, and ways to make them happy.

---

That night, Daniel recounted the day to Jake over dinner. He told him about following Alex around all day, Alex’s friends, and, of course, his talk with Alex.

“He wanted to know what my ‘whole deal’ was.”

Jake looked surprised. “Oh, did he? What did you tell him?”

“I don’t know, *I* don’t even know what my ‘whole deal’ is.”

“Yeah, I don’t think anyone knows that about themself. It just sounds like he was interested in you. Maybe he wants to be friends.”

“Maybe.”

Daniel thought again about what Alex’s friends had asked him. He looked at his uncle as he ate and wondered if he was gay. He wanted to ask him just then, but didn’t have any idea how to bring it up. Instead, he decided to ask about his parents.

“Why are my parents like that?”

Jake bugled his eyes. “I don’t think we have enough time to fully dissect that. That’s the real ‘whole deal’ we need to figure out.”

“I thought they were normal, but now I’m not so sure.”

“Usually it’s the normal people who are the most unusual.”

Daniel paused and reflected on this. He continued, “Like, why didn’t we ever visit you?”

“Well, your parents aren’t really so fond of me.”

“Oh. Well, I think you’re pretty great, so that’s doesn’t make any sense.”

“Thank you. You know, it’s probably time that I tell you, there’s a reason they aren’t so fond of me.” He paused. “It’s because I’m gay.”

The room suddenly became quiet, and the crunch of the carrot in Daniel’s mouth was so loud. He was speechless and just stared at his uncle. He didn’t expect him to offer up this information so soon.

Jake continued, “Your mom hated that I was gay. She just couldn’t handle it, so that was when we stopped seeing each other as much.”

“Oh.” Daniel shifted in his seat. “I didn’t know that.”

“I’m afraid that our stories are quite similar. When they kicked you out, it was a long time coming. I wish that I could have been there for you.”

Daniel’s heart hurt for his uncle, and for himself. He had been blaming himself for so much, but hearing that his mom could reject someone as kind and caring as his uncle, he became mad at his mom. She now seemed like a horrible person, and he couldn’t believe that she had done the same to them both.

“I don’t want to put more of a wedge between you and your parents, but you deserve to know the truth. I’m sorry, this is really heavy.”

“No, it’s okay. Everything is making way more sense now. I can’t believe that they’re like this.”

“I tried for a long time to make it work, but after a while, I just gave up. It was clear that it was how they wanted it. I’m afraid the same thing might be happening with you.”

“I don’t think I want to go back there anyway.”

“You deserve much, much better than that. I know I’ve said it before, but this is always a home for you. Nothing will ever change that as long as I’m around, okay?”

Daniel began to tear up. “I love you.”

“I love you too.”

---

Daniel worked at the café every other day that week, and by the end of the week, he was taking orders on his own and only occasionally asking Alex for help. He felt prouder of himself than he had in a long time, and ended up enjoying the job more than he had expected.

On Saturday, he worked the dinner shift, where the café stayed open later. He was excited to see what would be different. Alex wasn’t working, but a different waiter had come in named Claire.

The café was crowded, and Daniel and Claire hurried around to get everyone’s order and deliver food. Daniel was overwhelmed, but liked the focus that came from being so busy.

A table towards the front of the restaurant had two couples at it, and all of them ordered a glass of wine. As the night went on, they requested refills of their wine glasses, and Daniel obliged.

After a while, Daniel needed to open a new bottle of wine. In the back, he asked Claire if there was a bottle opener, and she pointed it out.

“This table at the front is really into the wine. I thought people just drank wine at church.”

Claire gave Daniel a look of confusion and left to deliver food to a table.

Back at the table, Daniel refilled two of the glasses. They thanked him profusely, and commented that the service that night had been better than usual, which made Daniel pleased. He considered that he was meant to do this job.

Later, Daniel was in the back, opening another bottle of wine. As he popped the cork, Claire entered.

“Oh, another bottle? Sheesh, we’re going through them tonight. Actually, wait, they know that refills aren’t free, right?”

“What?” Daniel asked, and then began to panic.

“You told them that they’d be charged for each glass, right?”

“No, I don’t know! I thought it was free refills!”

“Oh no. Oh, Daniel, no no no. How much have you given them?”

“Um...well, I guess two bottles of red and three of the white.”

“Oh god, okay. Well, let’s make this the last one, and let’s hope the owner isn’t too mad.”

“What do I tell them?”

“The owner? Well, let’s hope they understand that you’re young and inexperienced you don’t need to tell them anything.”

“No, I mean, what do I tell the table?”

“Oh, just tell them we’re out.”

“But we’re not.”

“Yeah, but lie.”

“I can’t lie, that’s bad.”

“Okay you need to chill out, it’s just a white lie. You have to lie to customers sometimes. What they don’t know won’t hurt them.”

Daniel covered his eyes and sighed. He took the bottle and followed Claire out to the dining room. At the table, he refilled the glasses and hoped that they didn’t say anything. Fortunately, they asked for the check, and Daniel left to ring them up.

---

The next day at the breakfast table, Daniel was still thinking about the wine. “I want to pay for it. I’m going to ask Brit how much I gave away, and then I’ll just pay it,” he said.

Jake bit into an almond croissant and then came to his defense. “You really don’t need to do that. It was an honest mistake.”

“Well, I should have known, it seems so stupid now that I’ve thought about it.” Daniel took a drink of his orange juice, his brow furrowed.

“If you didn’t know, they should have trained you better. They can’t expect you to know everything already.”

“If you say so.”

“Yes, I do. Don’t worry about this at all. In fact, I’m suspicious that the people at that table knew better and were taking advantage of you.”

“Wait, really?” Daniel paused to consider this. It hadn’t even occurred to him as a possibility. “Why would they do that?”

“Well, they got free wine out of it. Sometimes people are not entirely honest and they’ll see what they can get away with. You know, they won’t lie exactly, but they won’t be completely upfront about what they know.”

“I couldn’t imagine doing something like that.”

“That’s because you’re a good kid. If your parents instilled one good thing in you, it’s that. That being said, good kids can be a target. I’m afraid something like that will probably happen to you again. It’s good to trust people, but a little bit of suspicion can be a good thing.”

Daniel finished his bear claw. His new job had seemed so straightforward until now, but this was a twist. He wondered if this was the kind of thing that his parents were so scared of. All he knew was there were these mysterious bad things, but they never told him what they were. His uncle seemed to know about them, but he was still left worrying about his next shift.
