“Daniel! What a surprise!”

Daniel was standing on his Uncle Jake’s front porch, holding his bag, a look of shock on his face. His arrival had surprised him as well, he’d found his car driving the route. He’d only been there once before, when he was much younger. In fact, he hadn’t been sure that he even had the right house.

“Well, come in, come in!” Jake ushered him in.

Daniel stepped over the threshold. The house smelled sweet, like sugar and berries. His uncle was looking him up and down, taking him in, and Daniel couldn’t help doing the same to him. Jake had dark brown hair, a beard, and wore a pair of brown, round glasses.

Feeling awkward, Daniel wasn’t sure what to do next. Thankfully his uncle noticed. “Uh, you can leave your shoes over there.” Jake pointed towards a shoe mat behind the door. Daniel slipped off his shoes and lined them up next to a pair of his uncle’s.

“I was just about to have some dessert, would you want some?”

Daniel nodded and followed him down a narrow hallway and into the kitchen. His uncle’s house was very different from his own. It was much older, with dark wood trim throughout. The hardwood floor creaked as they walked. In the kitchen, Jake went to a cabinet and retrieved a small plate for Daniel. In the center of the room was a square table made of wood, and on it sat a pan of brownies.

“Do you like brownies? I know they aren’t everyone’s thing.” Jake grabbed an extra fork from the drawer.

Daniel set down his bag and took a seat. He noticed that the sweet smell of the brownies lingered in the air from being baked. Jake dished up one onto a plate and handed it over to Daniel, not waiting for his reply, and sat down as well. Daniel took the fork and broke off a small piece. The plate, he noticed, was light purple with a repeating floral pattern around the outside.

It tasted like nothing he’d had before. It was decadently rich, the chocolate frosting melting on his tongue. He took several more bites before he could stop himself. When his mom made dessert, which she rarely did, it was often bland and not very sweet.

Jake watched with curiosity, getting back to his own slice as he did. He smiled as Daniel ate like someone finding water in the desert. When he was done, Jake asked, “So, what’s going on?”

Daniel set down his fork and stared at his plate, inspecting the crumbs left there. He wanted to lick them up, but knew he shouldn’t. Finally, he mumbled, “I did something bad.”

“Okay.” Jake hesitated, and his brow furrowed. “Did you run away?”

Daniel paused and considered how much to say. “No. They kicked me out.”

“Your parents?”

“Yeah.” Daniel continued to inspect his plate, unable to meet his uncle’s eyes. He wanted to shrink into himself.

“Ah, I see.” Jake sighed. “I think... I think you came to the right place. Why don’t you stay here tonight? I’ve got an extra bedroom upstairs, and we can figure out the rest tomorrow.”

Daniel looked up from his plate. “Are you sure?”

“Of course. I will have to let your parents know that you’re here and safe, but my house is your house as long as you need it.”

Daniel pursed his lips. “I don’t think they’ll care, they were *really* mad at me.”

“I doubt that very much. I’ll still try to let them know you’re here in case they realize their mistake.”

Daniel took that in for a moment. *Their mistake?* he thought.

Jake got up from the table and motioned for Daniel to do the same. “Let me show you where it is. You haven’t been here in quite a while.” Daniel picked up his bag and followed.

They ascended a creaky staircase in the front hall. Along the wall were old portraits of different men. At the last one, Daniel noticed that he was shirtless and wearing joke glasses. At the top of the stairs, they crossed a hall and entered the bedroom.

“I know it’s not much, but hopefully this is comfortable for you,” Jake said.

Daniel stood and took in the room. There was a double bed in the center with a window on each side. On the bed was a dark green quilt with several pillows in different shades of cream. The walls were a light earthy green, and the room was filled with tchotchkes, artwork, and other colorful decor. On a desk sat a replica of _Little Dancer Aged Fourteen_. Next to the bed was an oversized dark brown accent chair. The light in the room was warm and inviting. It looked cozy, but still overwhelmed Daniel. It was so different from his own.

“Feel free to put your things in the dresser, just make yourself at home.”

Daniel set his bag down on the bed and sat down next to it. He slowly sunk into the soft mattress. He bounced up and down a few times to try it out.

“Oh, and let me show you where the bathroom is, it’s just next door.”

At the bathroom, Jake flipped on the light. Daniel noticed a citrus scent first, and wanted to go inside. Instead, he stood with his uncle and looked around. The room had white walls with gold accents. The vanity was light blue and a set of dark blue towels hung next to the shower. On the wall hung a framed picture of Jake standing in front of a mountain.

Daniel was quiet, taking it all in as best he could. Jake looked to him expectantly. “Think this will work?”

Daniel nodded.

“Okay then, how about another brownie?” Jake flipped off the light in the bathroom and they headed back downstairs.

In the kitchen, Jake scooped up another brownie and placed it on Daniel’s plate. Daniel noticed that he didn’t take one for himself. As he began to eat, Jake leaned on the table with his arms crossed. “So, do you want to talk about what happened?”

Daniel’s stomach sank. The kiss was on the tip of his lips, but he couldn’t bring himself to admit it out loud. He felt that it was safer to lean into his report card. He explained how he hadn’t been able to finish his math exam, how he’d messed up his perfect grades, and the harsh disappointment he’d gotten from his parents.

“I’m surprised by that, that doesn’t seem so bad to me,” Jake said.

“I don’t know, they were really mad about it all. I mean, usually Sarah and I get straight A’s, no problem. We’ve never messed up before. I mean, I’m still confused about how it happened.”

“You’re sure that that’s all that happened?”

Daniel nodded, continuing to work his way through the brownie. The second one was just as good as the first. He hoped that he could have brownies all the time. He thought that maybe if he kept eating brownies, he wouldn’t have to say anything else.

Jake let it go, and instead they got to talking about Daniel’s hobby of playing chess with Sarah. Then, Jake told Daniel about his job at a bank. Daniel learned more about it that evening than he had ever learned about his dad’s job.

When 9 o’clock arrived, Daniel announced that he needed to go to bed. Jake checked the clock and then looked at Daniel with confusion.

“You do? It’s still pretty early.”

“I always go to bed at 9.”

“Oh, okay. Well, I will see you in the morning then. If you need anything, just let me know.”

Daniel got up from the table and took his plate and fork to the dishwasher. Jake discretely watched in curiosity, fascinated by the rigidity of his nephew.

“Goodnight,” Daniel said as he left the kitchen.

Upstairs, he went into the guest room and turned on the light. It was a little less overwhelming than it had been before, but he stood for a moment looking around the room, taking it all in. He couldn’t explain why, but it felt like a safe place. In fact, maybe it was safer than any place he’d ever been.

He opened his bag and looked through the clothes that he had brought. There were several shirts, pants, underwear, and socks, but that was it. As he dug around, he realized that he’d forgotten to grab his pajamas, and he became frustrated. He looked around the room, hoping there might be some there. He went to the dresser and checked in the drawers, but found it full of blankets. They had fun patterns on them, which he tried to ignore.

He’d never slept without pajamas before, so he realized that he’d need to ask his uncle if he could borrow some. He went back down the staircase, each step squeaking in its own unique way.

He found that his uncle had moved to the living room at the front of the house. He was sitting on a sectional couch, his feet up on a coffee table. Daniel marveled at a massive TV sitting across from him.

“Hey, do you need something?” Jake asked.

“I didn’t bring any pajamas.”

“Oh!” Jake got up. “That’s quite alright, you can borrow some of mine. Come with me.”

Daniel followed him through the kitchen and then realized that they were at his room. He stopped in the hallway and backed away, but not before noticing that his uncle had a four poster bed with a colorful quilt on it. At home, he wasn’t allowed in his parents’ room and didn’t want to upset his uncle.

After a minute, Jake reappeared holding a pair of pajamas. The shirt was dark blue and had a graphic on it and the pants were plaid. “There you go. These will probably fit you best, you can try them on and see.”

Daniel softly took the pajamas. They matched the rest of his uncle’s house, but he’d never worn something like them before. They sort of terrified him, but he thanked his uncle and returned upstairs to his room.

He changed into the pajamas and looked at himself in the floor length mirror that hung on the wall next to the dresser.

The graphic on the shirt was of two outlined men dancing under a red heart, and the plaid of the pants was red and blue. In the mirror was someone he didn’t recognize, and it scared him. The events of the day started to play in his head and he turned from the mirror. At the bed, he wiped a tear from his eye and moved his bag to the floor.

He pulled down the covers and slipped into bed. Before settling in, he realized that the lights were still on, so he got back up and flipped the switch. Back in bed, he nestled in the best that he could. He had to admit that everything felt more comfortable than he’d experienced before. The pajamas were soft on his skin. He could tell that his uncle had worn them a lot, which was comforting and unsettling all at once.

He tried to fall asleep, but thoughts continued to rattle around in his mind. His parents were yelling at him for the kiss, they were yelling at him for his report card, he was searching for his uncle’s house up and down the street, he was kissing Oliver, he was eating a brownie, he was kissing Oliver, he was asking his uncle for pajamas, his sister was smiling at her report card, Oliver was staring at him in the equipment room.

He chastised himself for failing to fall asleep. He was supposed to be asleep so that he would get eight hours and then wake up on time. He was already messing up, so his uncle would probably wish that he would leave. He would have to go back to his parents and they would yell at him again and again. Finally, after midnight, he fell into an unsettled sleep.

Daniel woke to knocking at the door. He struggled to open his eyes, groggy with sleep. He was on his stomach, he realized, and rotated as he lifted himself up. His uncle was saying something on the other side of the door.

“...making breakfast, are you awake yet?”

“Yeah,” he mumbled, hoping he’d been loud enough.

“Okay, can I open the door?”

“Sure,” Daniel managed. He sat up in bed and rubbed his eyes.

Jake opened the door and peeked in. “Sorry, did I wake you up?”

“It’s okay,” he mumbled again.

“Well, I was just making omelets, I wanted to see if you wanted one.”

Daniel furrowed his brow. “What’s an omelet?”

“Oh, well why don’t you come try one then!” As he spoke, Jake opened the door further and Daniel realized that he was only wearing a pair of green boxers. His chest and belly were covered in dark, curly hair. He knew that he shouldn’t look, but he’d never seen a man with his shirt off before. He looked down at the bed, then looked back at his uncle’s face, trying to avoid looking any lower.

“Come on, I’ll put whatever you want in it,” Jake said.

Daniel got up from the bed and followed. As they went down the stairs, he noticed that his uncle’s back had the same hair on it, and he allowed himself to look at it while his uncle couldn’t see him. He didn’t know that men were that hairy.

In the kitchen, Jake returned to the stove, where he checked the bottom of an omelet that was already cooking. He turned to look at Daniel. “Let’s see, do you like goat cheese?”

“Um, I don’t know.” Daniel took a seat at the table. He was still unsure exactly how to look at his uncle, but was so curious.

“Okay, I think I have some cheddar, is that okay?”

“Yeah.” He watched as his uncle went to the fridge, pulled out the package of cheese, and then went back to the stove.

Jake turned to him again with an inquisitive look. “What about mushrooms?”

“I don’t know.” Daniel pursed his lips.

“Okay! Well, I’ll just make you a plain one then, it’ll still be good.”

Once it was done, Jake removed the omelet from the pan and plated it, then poured in the mixture for Daniel’s. Daniel continued to take in the kitchen. He hadn’t noticed last night, but the kitchen had coordinating orange accents throughout. The toaster was a bright shade and sat in the middle of the counter. The walls were covered with a patterned wallpaper, with vertical squiggly lines and other repeating flourishes. On one wall was a painting of an orange van.

When Daniel’s omelet was ready, Jake brought them both to the table and they started to eat. Jake poured two glasses of orange juice from a bottle that was already on the table.

“So, I have to go to work today. Are you going to be okay staying here for the day?” Jake asked.

“Um, yeah, I’ll be okay.” Daniel hadn’t been so unsure of anything in his life.

“I’ll be back for lunch to make us something to eat. I should be able to get away at about noon. In the meantime, make yourself at home, there’s TV and books and anything else you can find in the living room. Just have a look around.”

After his uncle left, Daniel went back up to his room. He wanted to shower, but the bathroom seemed too overwhelming to figure out yet. He sat back down on the bed and then laid down on his side. The house had a new stillness about it. It was eerily quiet and loud at the same time and it consumed him.

He wondered what his parents were doing. *Had they just gone on like usual without him?* he wondered. He thought of his sister, whether she was missing him or not. He missed her, he thought, but then a disturbing idea arrived that he didn’t.

He felt lost, like he was adrift in the ocean, bobbing around without any land in sight. He let himself sink back into his new bed, and into the unknown new world of his uncle’s.
