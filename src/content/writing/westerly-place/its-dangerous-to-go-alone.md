“I just feel like Eric is so inauthentic. Like, does anybody actually believe in ghosts?” Catherine was exasperated.

Nick sighed. “Yes, we’ve been over this, some people really do. They think they’re talking to their grandma or whatever.”

Daniel was at an Italian restaurant with Alex and his friends, and they were discussing the last episode of *Phantomville* while they waited for their pasta to arrive. Daniel had never heard of the show, but was listening with intent anyway.

“What about you Daniel, you believe in ghosts?” Catherine asked.

Daniel involuntarily blushed at the attention. “Oh, no, I don’t think so.”

“See, Daniel gets it. This is why you have to watch this show with us!” Catherine exclaimed.

“Maybe. I don’t really watch any TV at all, we didn’t have a TV when I was growing up.”

Confusion took over Catherine’s face. “Never had a TV?! Are you serious? I would die.”

“Doesn’t your uncle have a TV?” Nick asked.

“Yeah, he does, he’s usually in his office though. We haven’t watched anything before.”

In this light, Daniel wasn’t sure how to tell them that this was the first time that he’d been out with a group of friends to a restaurant. His parents had allowed some of his friends to come over for short visits, but that was it.

Jake had been very excited that Alex had invited him out. Despite that, Daniel was really nervous about it, and had kind of hoped that his uncle might say no. He was curious what it would be like, but Alex’s friends seemed so cool and worldly, he wasn’t sure why they wanted to hang out with him anyway.

Catherine was back on the show. “So what do we think is going to happen on tonight’s episode? Will we find out who the evil mastermind is?”

Alex thought for a moment. “I don’t think so. This isn’t the season finale, so it’s probably not going to wrap anything up yet.”

“Good point, hadn’t thought of that. So more disappearing townspeople it is.”

Alex laughed. “For sure. The barber is definitely next, he’s been getting so many close calls.”

Catherine looked over to Daniel. “Guys, I think we’re boring Daniel, his eyes are glazing over.”

“Oh, no, sorry! I’m just trying to keep up.”

Catherine leaned forward. “So, Daniel, tell us, are you crushing on anyone right now?”

Daniel involuntarily blushed, again. “Um, no, not right now.”

“Catherine, stop,” Alex said, annoyed.

“Anyone at this table, maybe?” she continued, and then looked over at Alex.

“Stop!” Alex demanded.

“Okay, just asking.” Catherine held up her hands and leaned back in her seat. “Just curious, no worries.” Her face showed that she’d gotten the answer she was looking for.

Daniel looked around at everyone’s faces, trying to figure out what they had meant. He wondered if they thought he had a crush on one of them. He had never come out to them, officially, but he wondered if they knew anyway. 

Thankfully, he didn’t need to wonder any longer, as their food arrived. Daniel had never been more relieved to see a dish of ravioli before.

After that night, Daniel was invited to hang out with Alex and his friends more frequently. They met at local parks, went out to eat, and he visited their houses. He still felt like an outsider, but enjoyed getting to know them. He was fascinated by their families and soaked up how they all lived. So many things were familiar to him, but completely different from how he had grown up.

As the calendar turned to August, Daniel realized that he should invite them over to his uncle’s house. He knew that they would love his uncle, but didn’t know what they would do there for fun. He knew that his job at the café would be ending soon, and he wasn’t sure how much he’d be able to see them at school.

Daniel asked his uncle, and again, his uncle was more excited that he had expected. He immediately began planning the meal and coming up with activities that they could all do afterwards. They decided to invite them over that Friday night.

---

The doorbell rang, and Daniel rushed from the kitchen to answer the door. Out on the stoop stood Catherine, Nick, and Alex. They were all smiles as Daniel opened the door. He ushered them inside, and led them back to the kitchen.

Daniel’s uncle was busy finishing up the food, but came to greet them and invited them to sit at the table to wait.

“Daniel, this is so exciting!” Catherine said.

Alex had sat next to Daniel and nudged him. Quietly, he said, “Your uncle’s place is really cool.”

Daniel smiled and said, “Thanks.”

The group talked about returning to school as they waited. Alex, Catherine, and Nick would be seniors in high school, while Daniel would be a junior. Daniel’s uncle listened carefully but remained quiet as he prepped the food.

Soon, Daniel’s uncle brought over a tray with what he called “kitchen sink veggie burgers,” and everyone took one onto their plate. He also brought over some roasted veggies and a salad.

Daniel was surprised about what his uncle had made. He had never made anything like this before. Everything had vegetables in it. No one said anything about it, so Daniel picked up his burger and tried it. He was amazed to find that it was full of flavor.

“Do you like it?” Jake asked, upon seeing his reaction.

Daniel nodded and smiled with his mouth full. “Mhmm,” he managed.

“Yeah, this is really good,” Catherine added. The rest of the table made sounds of agreement.

“Usually when my mom makes these, she leaves them plain. These are great.” Nick said.

“I’m glad you like them!” Daniel’s uncle beamed. “So, are you all into anything on TV right now?”

Daniel was the only one not chewing, so he offered up, “Phantomville!”

Jake looked surprised. “Oh, are you watching that?”

“No, I’ve just heard them talking about it.”

“I see. Well what did you all think of that finale?”

Catherine set her burger down. “I can’t believe it was a rogue AI the whole time! Such a twist.”

“I know! I kind of suspected it though,” Jake replied.

“Wait, you did?”

“Yeah, that whole dream sequence where the characters looked distorted? I think that was meant to be from the point of view of the AI.”

“Ohhh, I didn’t even think of that!” Catherine replied.

“That’s such a good point,” Nick said.

Alex made a look of realization. “I thought that was meant to show what the phantom’s could see, but that makes way more sense.”

Daniel’s uncle wiped his mouth with a napkin. “What do you all think the cliffhanger meant though?”

Nick sat up in his seat. “It means next season is going to be a musical!”

Everyone laughed. “Not everything is a musical, you idiot,” Catherine teased.

Daniel was beginning to feel out of body. His friends were connecting with his uncle in a way that he hadn’t yet. He felt jealous of them, and tried thinking of a way to change the subject.

Fortunately, his uncle was already on it. “So you’re all going to be seniors this year?”

Everyone but Daniel nodded.

“I’m glad Daniel’s going to have some friends at school, this will be his first year there.” He looked at Daniel as he spoke. “It’s going to be really hard to switch schools in the middle of high school.”

Daniel looked at his plate. He wasn’t sure what to say.

“We’ll look after him,” Catherine offered. It sounded half-hearted to Daniel. He didn’t want to be looked after anyway, he thought.

After they finished eating, Daniel’s uncle brought out some cookies, and then he left them to retreat to his office.

As they settled into the living room, Catherine said, “Your uncle is super cool.”

“Yeah, I think so too,” Daniel replied.

“I can’t believe he watches *Phantomville*, that’s so random.”

Daniel got out a card game and asked if everyone wanted to play. They gathered around the coffee table on the floor and Daniel passed the cards out. They played for an hour, and Daniel won most of the rounds.

Once everyone had left, Alex went to find his uncle. His office was upstairs, across from Daniel’s room. He stopped in the doorway and waited.

“Everyone take off?” Jake said, still facing his computer.

“Yeah. Curfew.”

Jake spun around in his chair. “I’m so glad they came over, we’ll have to do that again.”

Daniel didn’t say anything and looked uncomfortable.

“...or not. Something bothering you?”

“What does it mean to have a crush?”

Jake motioned for him to come into the room and sit down. Daniel took a spot on the couch next to the desk.

“Well, a crush is when you’re really excited to be around someone. You don’t really know why, but they’re special to you. Why do you ask?”

“Catherine was asking if I had one on anyone. I think I do.”

“Oh?”

“Yeah, I think I have a crush on Alex.” Daniel hid his face behind his hands.

Jake smiled. “That’s very exciting, I’m so happy for you.”

Daniel lowered his hands and groaned. “Why? It’s not like it will turn into anything.”

“Hey, don’t talk like that. You don’t know what will happen. Plus, I could tell that you two had something special.”

Daniel blushed. “Wait. What?”

“Yeah. You were smiling at each other through the whole meal.”

“No we weren’t!”

“Oh yes you were.” Jake laughed. “It was really sweet.”

“He just terrifies me. Like, I don’t think I’m anywhere cool enough for him.”

Jake got up from his chair and came over to the couch. He sat down right next to Daniel and put his arm around him.

“Just because this is all new to you doesn’t mean you aren’t cool. You may find that you’re cooler than the rest of them. Your superpower is that you’re an outsider. You see things that no one else can. Now, that does mean that things are harder for you, but they’ll get easier over time.”

Daniel didn’t quite understand what his uncle meant, but it sounded promising.

“You should ask Alex out on a date,” Jake proposed.

“What? No. No no no no.”

“Yes yes yes. What’s the worst that could happen? You spill cranberry juice on his brand new white oxford shirt and call him an idiot on accident?”

“That’s oddly specific.”

“Yeah, that might be because it happened to me.”

Daniel put his hands over his mouth and laughed. “Oh no!”

“Oh yes. We did *not* have a second date.”

“Oh, that’s awkward. I would die of embarrassment.”

“And I did.”

They laughed and snuggled together on the couch, reveling in each other’s company.

Later that night, Daniel went into the bathroom to get ready for bed. He brushed his teeth, and while he looked at himself in the mirror, he pulled up his shirt to look at his belly. He was pleased to see that he’d lost most of the weight he’d put on that summer. He remembered how sad that he’d been about it, but after he’d talked to his uncle, he hadn’t thought about it as much.

He spat out the toothpaste, washed his face, and left the bathroom.

In bed that night, Daniel thought about the summer that he’d had. It had been the best summer of his life. Getting kicked out of his parents house might have been the best thing to happen to him. He tried not to think about what might have happened had he not had his uncle to go to, and his heart broke for other kids with nowhere to go.

He thought about Oliver, and wondered if he was safe. Did his parents know that he was gay? Or maybe he was bisexual. He wondered if he would ever find out. Despite all of the fun that he’d had that summer, there was still a darkness that loomed, and it seemed to be sticking around no matter what happened. He began to cry, and tried to think about something else.
