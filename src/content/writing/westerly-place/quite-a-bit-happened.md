“You look like you’ve seen a ghost.”

Jake had just come into the living room to find Daniel uncomfortably sitting on the couch, waiting to leave for his date with Alex.

Daniel looked at his uncle and tried to relax his face. “Oh, do I? Well, good thing I don’t believe in ghosts then.” He laughed, halfheartedly.

Jake sat down next to him. “Are you worried about your date?”

Daniel looked at his hands. “I guess, kinda.”

“Hey, that’s okay. Don’t pressure yourself too much though. It’s probably going to be awkward and scary, this is your first one.”

Daniel nodded, continuing to look at his hands. They were clammy and felt weird.

“Plus, Alex is probably feeling the same way. And you’re already friends, so you’ve got the hard part out of the way.”

“What if he wants to kiss?”

Jake smiled. “Do you want to kiss him?”

Daniel thought for a moment and his cheeks reddened. “Yeah,” he admitted.

“There you go,” Jake said. “Don’t forget about what you want too.”

Daniel looked at him and Jake noticed that he looked lighter.

He continued, “And if something goes wrong, it’s okay to leave. I’ll be here for you.”

“Thanks, Uncle Jake,” Daniel replied, bashfully.

“Well, you better be getting on your way. I’ll see you when you get back!”

Daniel got up off the couch, Jake brought him in for a hug, and then he left the house.

At the café, he walked through the front door to find that Alex was already at a table along the side of the dining room. The lights were low and glowing candles at each table made the room cozy. There were only a handful of other occupied tables, which put him at ease.

“Hey,” Daniel said as he arrived at the table. He took his seat and looked at Alex. He looked more radiant than he ever had. They had been in this café together on countless nights that summer, but none of them had been like this.

“Hey. I went ahead and ordered waters for us. The staff tonight seems terrible though.” Alex deadpanned.

Daniel looked to the back of the room to see Catherine coming towards them with two glasses of water. “Wait, is that Catherine?”

Alex looked back as Catherine approached. She set the waters down without a word and then left.

Daniel was mystified. “Since when does she work here?” he asked.

“Since tonight!” Alex replied. “And I think it might be a one night only kind of thing, she’s super unqualified.”

Daniel laughed. “That’s amazing!”

After a moment, Alex continued. “I’m so glad you asked me out.”

“I’m glad you said yes. I’ve never been on a date before.”

“Well, now you have!”

They each looked down at the menu and pretended to pick out what they wanted to eat. Each of them already knew.

Soon, Catherine returned. “So, what can I get you boys?” She had a pencil stuck behind her ear that she retrieved as she pulled a notepad from her pocket.

They each told her their order and she disappeared again.

“I can’t believe this is happening,” Daniel said, still delighted with Catherine.

“I had to beg her not to do an accent, so consider yourself lucky.”

Daniel laughed again. He was relieved that the date seemed to be going well.

After the meal, they stepped outside of the café. Alex looked hesitant, and then motioned for Daniel to follow him around the corner of the building. There, out of eyesight of the customers inside, he turned to face Daniel.

“Can I kiss you?”

Ever so slightly, Daniel nodded. His heart was already racing. Alex leaned in and placed his lips on Daniel’s. He was gentle at first, and Daniel couldn’t believe how good it felt. He leaned in and they kissed with more meaning. After a moment, they pulled apart and looked into each other’s eyes.

Alex smiled with glee and Daniel joined in. They kissed one more time, and Daniel didn’t want it to end. When Alex pulled away, Daniel looked awkward.

“Well, I better go. I liked this. A lot,” Alex said.

Daniel giggled. “Me too. I guess I’ll see you at school.”

“Yeah. See you at school.” With this, Alex turned to leave. Daniel watched him for a bit, then turned to leave himself. He didn’t notice, but Alex turned to glance back at him, a smile still on his face.

Back at home, Daniel found his uncle watching old episodes of *Phantomville* in the living room. He sat up when Daniel entered.

“Well?”

Daniel couldn’t speak, just smiled a goofy grin and jumped up and down. Jake joined him and scooped him up in a big hug, making Daniel’s feet leave the floor.

“I’m so happy for you!” Jake said.

“Thank you. I’m so relieved, it went really well.”

“Did anything, um, special happen?” Jake asked, expectantly.

Daniel bit his lip and smiled. “Yeah. We kissed.”

Jake fell back onto the couch with his arms out. “This is amazing.” As Daniel was about to sit down too, Jake jumped up. “Come on, we’re going to celebrate.” He led them into the kitchen, where he pulled out a little red velvet cake from the fridge. He cut off two squares for the both of them and they took them into the living room to eat.

---

In October, Jake came up with an idea for Daniel. He thought that it would be good for Daniel to return to his high school for one of his sister’s volleyball games, in an attempt to see her, his friend Oliver, and possibly his parents. He presented this over dinner on a Thursday night.

When he was finished, he asked, “So, what do you think?”

“I don’t know, I don’t think I’m ready yet.”

“I worried about that, and I don’t want to push you into it. But I’m also really worried about your relationship with your sister, because I don’t want you to lose it in all of this. And, I think that if you were to get to see Oliver, you might be able to get some closure on what happened between you.”

“I do really miss Sarah. I mean, we used to spend every waking moment together. She hasn’t even tried to contact me though.”

“On that, I can’t begin to imagine what your parents have been telling her. I think that’s why it’s important that you see her. You can show her that you’re still the same person.”

“Yeah, you’re right.” Daniel paused, trying to imagine what it would be like. “I don’t even know *what* I’d say to Oliver.”

Jake was quiet, letting him think on his own. Daniel took a bite of the pot roast they were eating.

In a singsong voice, Daniel said, “‘Hey, remember that time you kissed me? It kind of wrecked my life!’”

Jake chuckled. “Well, that’s one way to put it. I don’t think that he wrecked your life though. That was all on your parents.”

“True, I shouldn’t make Oliver feel bad. Kissing him was nice.”

---

The day of the game arrived and Jake and Daniel made the trip. In the parking lot, Jake put the car in park and looked over at Daniel. “Ready?” he asked.

“Nope!” Daniel replied gleefully.

“Okay, great, here we go.”

They got out of the car and headed towards the entrance. As they walked, Jake put his hand on Daniel’s shoulder and squeezed. Inside the lobby, there was a crowd. They made their way through to the gym entrance and emerged next to the bleachers. Daniel had been there countless times before, but it felt very different now. The harsh restrictions of his childhood crowded back into his mind.

They found a seat about a third of the way up and sat down. They both took in the room and watched as people milled about and found their seats. Daniel looked for Sarah, and finally found her sitting in the front row of the bleachers with the rest of her team. After a few minutes, they all stood up and Sarah looked around and her eyes met Daniel’s. She immediately ran up to him.

“Daniel! You’re here!” She took him in a hesitant hug. “Hi, Uncle Jake.” She waved to Jake.

Daniel wasn’t sure what to say. “It’s so good to see you.”

“You too. I miss you so much, I didn’t know when I would ever see you again.”

“I miss you too. I’m sorry I haven’t reached out, you know.”

“No, I know. I’m sorry too. Can we start talking again?”

“Yeah, I’d love that. I have so much to tell you.”

“Me too. Okay, I should go, I’m supposed to be warming up. I’ll find you after!”

“Okay, good luck!”

“Thanks!”

Daniel sat down again. He couldn’t believe that he’d just spoken to his sister again, and tried to let it sink in.

“She misses me,” he said aloud.

Jake rubbed his back. “Yeah, she does. She still cares about you.”

Daniel was overwhelmed, but then looked down to see his parents entering at the other end of the gym and it kicked into overdrive. They were too far away to notice him, but he wanted to hide. Jake noticed him looking at them and looked over too.

“Okay, they’re here. You doing okay?” Jake asked.

“Yeah. Yeah, I’m fine,” Daniel lied. He had a bad feeling in the pit of his stomach. He looked back at Sarah warming up.

“Okay good. I’m here with you. And remember what I said, if you get too uncomfortable, we’ll leave. You don’t have to say anything to them if you don’t want to.”

“Thank you. I don’t know how I would do this without you.”

“You’d probably be throwing up a blue ICEE in the bathroom.”

Daniel laughed. He probably would be, he thought.

The game started soon after and Daniel watched intently as his sister played. He was so proud of her, she had grown so much since he’d seen her last.

At halftime, Daniel and Jake got up and headed to the concessions stand. There, the found a long line, which they joined.

“You doing okay?” Jake asked.

“Yeah, I’m good.” That was when Daniel realized that the cashier was Oliver. “Actually, a little nervous.” He watched Oliver and hoped that he wouldn’t notice. He looked happy, like everything was fine.

Once they reached the front of the line, Oliver realized it was him.

“Oh hey, you’re back! Where have you been?”

“Oh, um, I live with my uncle now.” Daniel gestured to Jake.

“Two hot dogs, please,” Jake said.

As Oliver tapped on the screen, he looked to Daniel again. “Gotcha, did something happen?”

Daniel didn’t want to get into it, so offered, “Can we talk later? Sit with us when you’re done?”

“Yeah! I’ll find you.”

Jake paid for the food and then they moved down the counter to wait for their food. Daniel looked at Jake, who made a face of acknowledgement. *Oliver didn’t know what happened after the kiss*, he realized.

Once they received their food, they turned around to head back to the gym. As they came around a corner, they saw Daniel’s parents approaching from the other direction. It was unavoidable, they would have to say something. They all stopped as they reached each other. Daniel’s mom was the first to speak.

“Hi Daniel.” She was expressionless and straightened her jacket.

“Hi Mom,” he replied.

“How are you,” his dad said, asking a question but making it sound like a statement.

“I’m good.” Daniel looked to Jake, who placed his arm around Daniel’s shoulders. “Uncle Jake has been taking care of me.”

“Good.” His mom said. She looked to Jake. “Do you need money?”

Jake tried not to show any emotions on his face, then said, “No, we’re all good, don’t worry about that. Um, Daniel has something that he’d like to say though.”

Daniel looked down for a moment, then blurted out, “I can’t believe you did that to me. I’ll never forgive you.” He immediately turned and left.

Jake apologized for him and then followed. As he caught up to Daniel, he said, “Hey, hey, you okay?”

Daniel was fighting back tears and walking quickly. “Yep. I’m fine.”

Back at their seats, Daniel wiped at his face and opened his hot dog.

Jake looked down, trying to find something to say. “I’m proud of you for standing up for yourself.”

“I can’t believe they don’t care about me at all. All they asked about was money. Who cares about money? I’m their freaking son.”

Jake rubbed his back and pulled him close. “I know. It’s a horrible way to be.”

“I should have run away a long time before they kicked me out. I can’t believe I lived with them for so long.”

“Hey, you didn’t know any better. And now you do.” He turned to look at Daniel. “Are you okay to stay? We can leave if you want to.”

“No, it’s okay. If they don’t care about me, I don’t care about them.”

They watched as the game resumed and Sarah served the ball.

After twenty minutes had passed, Daniel noticed that Oliver had appeared at the bottom of the bleachers and waved him up.

“Hey dude!” Oliver said as he sat down next to Daniel.

“All done with your shift?”

“Kinda, I sorta ditched them to come talk to you.”

“Oh.” Daniel looked flushed.

They watched the game for a little bit, and then Oliver broke the silence. “So, why are you living with your uncle?”

“Well, basically, my parents kicked me out.”

“Whoa. What for?”

“I messed up a bunch of my grades, so they were mad about that, and then I sort of told them about what happened between us.”

Oliver went silent and looked worried. He looked over at Jake, scared that he had overheard.

“Oh, Uncle Jake knows, it’s okay.”

Uncle Jake gave Oliver a friendly wave and smile and then went back to watching the game. He was still listening intently, though.

“Oh, okay,” Oliver said, hesitantly. Then he realized his part in what happened. “I’m so, so sorry, I didn’t mean to—”

“No, no, it’s okay.” Daniel reassured him. “I’m glad you did it.”

“Oh.” Oliver looked surprised and then the slightest smile appeared. “Good. So you aren’t coming back to school?”

“No, I’m going to a school in the city.”

Oliver frowned. “Gotcha. Well, I miss you a lot.”

“I miss you too. Do you think we can we still be friends?”

“Are you goofy? Of course. We should hang out sometime. Can you come over to my house?”

“If my uncle says it’s—”

“He can!” Jake interjected. Daniel and Oliver looked over at him and laughed, realizing that he’d very much been paying attention.

---

In the car on the way home, Daniel and Jake talked about everything that had happened that night. The sun had set, so their faces were only lit by the dashboard lights.

“Thanks for taking me tonight. That got kind of out of control, but it was good,” Daniel said.

“Good. I’m glad it worked out okay. I’m proud of you, you were really brave for doing that. I’ll always be proud of you.” He took Daniel’s hand and squeezed it.

Daniel started to tear up. “Thanks, Uncle Jake.”

“And look, you still have a sister and a friend. They still care about you, very much.”

“Yeah.” Daniel smiled. “I didn’t realize how much I’ve been missing them. I used to talk to both of them every single day.” Daniel paused for a moment. “Should I have told Oliver that I have a boyfriend?”

“You have a boyfriend?!”

“Don’t I? Isn’t Alex my boyfriend?”

“Oh, I didn’t know you had made it official yet.”

“Hmm, maybe I should ask him about that.”

Jake smiled. “Yes you should.”
